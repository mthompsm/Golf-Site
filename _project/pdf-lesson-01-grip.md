# Lesson 1: Grip That Controls the Clubface

## Why Grip Comes First

If you take nothing else from this guide, take this: the grip is the most
important fundamental in golf. Not the swing. Not your stance. The grip.

Here is why. The clubface controls where the ball goes. And your hands are
the only connection between you and the clubface. If that connection is
wrong, nothing else you do — no swing thought, no drill, no adjustment —
will produce consistent results. You are literally building on a broken
foundation.

The other thing I want to say upfront: the correct grip often feels
awkward. Especially at first. Especially if you have been gripping the club
the wrong way for years. That awkward feeling is not a sign that something
is wrong. It is a sign that something is finally right. Stick with it.

---

## The Process

This is how I build my grip every single time I step up to the ball. Not
sometimes. Every time. After thousands of repetitions the process becomes
faster — almost automatic — but the process never changes. That consistency
is exactly the point.

### Step 1 — Start with the Club Face

Hold the club out in front of you with your right hand, arm extended, and
look at the face. Make sure it is square — not open, not closed. This is
your reference point. Everything that follows is built around a square face.
Do not skip this step. It takes two seconds and it anchors everything else.

### Step 2 — Left Hand (Lead Hand)

Bring your left hand under the club. Place the grip diagonally across your
fingers — not in the palm, across the fingers. This is important. A palm
grip kills your ability to hinge the wrists correctly.

Once your fingers are positioned, wrap your hand around the club and bring
your thumb to rest on top of the shaft. I personally like the feeling of
extending my left thumb slightly down the shaft — it creates a more secure
connection and gives me a reference point I can feel every time.

Your left hand V — the channel formed between your thumb and index finger —
should point toward your left shoulder. This is your check. If it is
pointing toward your chin or your right shoulder, your grip is too weak and
the face will tend to open at impact.

### Step 3 — Right Hand (Trail Hand)

Place your right hand on the club so that your right pinky finger sits over
the gap between your left index finger and middle finger. You can overlap it
or interlock it — either works. What matters is that the hands feel
connected and unified, not separate.

Now turn your right hand over so that your left thumb sits in the pocket of
your right palm. The two hands should feel like one unit.

Two things I personally focus on with my right hand:

**The trigger finger.** My right index finger sits slightly away from the
others, bent — like I am pulling a trigger. This gives me a sense of
control and feel through the shot.

**The last two fingers.** I focus on light tension in the ring finger and
pinky of my right hand against the shaft. Not a death grip. Light, firm
tension. These two fingers are your connection. Feel them on every swing.

Your right hand V should point toward your right shoulder. Both Vs — left
and right — working in the same direction is what gives you a consistent,
repeatable grip.

---

## How Tight to Grip

This is one of the most common questions and the answer is simpler than
most people make it. Grip the club firmly enough that it will not twist in
your hands at impact — but not so tight that you feel tension in your
forearms. If your forearms are tense, you are gripping too hard. Tension
kills feel and restricts the natural rotation of the forearms through
impact.

Think of it as a 5 or 6 on a scale of 10. Secure, not strangled.

---

## Same Grip for Every Club

One of the most damaging habits I see in recreational golfers is changing
the grip between clubs. Loosening up for the driver, tightening for a short
iron, adjusting for a chip. Every change introduces a new variable and a
new opportunity for inconsistency.

Build one grip. Use it for every club. The swing changes slightly between
clubs — the grip does not.

---

## Practice Until It Is Automatic

The grip is a physical skill. Reading about it is not enough. You need to
build it the same way, in the same sequence, hundreds of times until the
process is so ingrained that you stop thinking about it.

Here is how I suggest you practice it:

- Grip and re-grip the club while watching television
- Before every shot on the range, build the grip from Step 1
- Check your Vs after each grip until checking becomes unnecessary
- Do not move on to the next lesson until building a correct grip feels
  natural — not easy, just natural

It will take time. That is fine. Time spent on the grip is the highest
return investment you can make in your golf game.

---

## The Most Common Mistake

A grip that is too weak — hands rotated too far toward the target — is the
single most common grip error in recreational golf. A weak grip leaves the
face open at impact. An open face at impact produces slices, pushes, and
loss of distance. If you have been fighting a slice for years, there is a
very good chance your grip is too weak.

The fix is to strengthen the grip slightly — rotate both hands away from the
target until both Vs point where they should. It will feel wrong at first.
Hit balls anyway. Within a session or two, the ball flight will tell you
everything you need to know.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Start with a square face every single time — this is non-negotiable
- Lead hand diagonal across fingers, V toward left shoulder
- Trail hand over lead thumb, V toward right shoulder
- Trigger finger feel on right index finger
- Light tension in last two fingers of right hand
- Same grip for every club, every shot
- Practice the process until it is automatic — not just familiar
