Foundation

The Full Swing

A reliable, repeatable swing is built from sequenced positions, not forced power. Master each phase and consistency becomes automatic.

🏌️ The Full Swing

Building a Repeatable Swing

The golf swing is an athletic, rotational movement powered primarily by the large muscles of the core and hips. Common beginner mistakes — lifting arms, swinging with just the hands — are corrected by understanding the sequence: feet, hips, torso, arms, hands. Everything fires in that order on the downswing.

The key checkpoint is the "halfway back" position: the club shaft should be parallel to the ground, toe of the club pointing up, and your left arm (for right-handers) straight across the chest. From here, complete the shoulder turn to 90°.

1 Address & posture: Bend from the hips (not the waist), soft knee flex, weight on the balls of your feet. Spine stays neutral — no hunching or arching.
2 One-piece takeaway: Move the club, arms, and shoulders away as a single unit for the first 18 inches. Avoid cocking the wrists early.
3 Top of backswing: Shoulder turn of 90°, hip turn of 45°, left arm relatively straight. Feel the "X-factor" stretch between hips and shoulders.
4 The transition: Starts with the lower body. Push off the right heel, shift weight left, then let the arms "drop" into the slot — never throw from the top.
5 Impact: Hips clear, hands ahead of the ball, weight predominantly on the front foot. The divot begins at or just after the ball — never before it.
6 Follow-through & finish: Rotate to a full, balanced finish — belt buckle facing the target, weight fully on front foot, right heel off the ground. Hold it.
Short Game

The Art of Putting

The flatstick is responsible for nearly half your score. Small improvements here have an outsized impact on your handicap.

🕳️ On the Green

Sink More Putts, Every Round

Great putters share three things: a stable, repeatable stroke; accurate green reading; and confident distance control. The stroke itself is simple — a pendulum motion driven by the shoulders, with minimal wrist involvement. It is the mind that gets in the way.

Coach Mike's key putting rule: speed before line. If you have the right pace, a slightly offline putt will often lip in. A perfectly aimed putt with wrong speed will never go in.

1 Setup: Eyes directly over the ball or just inside it. Feet shoulder-width apart. Bend from the hips until arms hang naturally. Grip lightly.
2 The stroke: Rock the shoulders like a pendulum. The putter head moves straight back and through on short putts; naturally arcs on longer ones. Wrists stay quiet.
3 Reading the green: Walk the full length of the putt. Crouch at the low side of the break — you'll see more slope from there. Note the grain direction.
4 Distance control: Calibrate by tempo, not force. A longer backstroke at the same pace gives more distance. Practice 20-foot putts within a 3-foot circle.
5 Pre-putt routine: Pick a spot 6 inches in front of the ball on your intended line. Aim at that spot — it's far easier to align to a close target.
Short Game

Chipping Around the Green

A sharp short game turns bogeys into pars. Learn to get up and down from any lie within 30 yards of the green.

🌿 Around the Green

Up and Down Every Time

Chipping is often overcomplicated. At its core, a chip is a mini version of the full swing: narrow stance, weight forward, hands ahead at address — and keep them there through impact. The chip runs along the ground more than it flies, so landing zone selection is everything.

The "land it on the green and let it roll" principle simplifies club selection. Pick your landing spot first, then choose the club that gets you there with the right ratio of air to roll.

1 Club selection rule: Use the lowest-lofted club that clears the rough/fringe and lands on the putting surface. A 7-iron chip runs further than a pitching wedge chip — use it.
2 Setup: Narrow, open stance. Ball back in stance (toward right foot). Hands pressed forward — shaft leans toward the target before you even start.
3 The motion: Small, controlled rocking of the shoulders. Weight stays on the front foot throughout — no shifting. Think "putting stroke with a lofted club."
4 Tight lies: Grip down on the club, stand closer, keep the leading edge low at address. Accelerate through — never decelerate into a tight lie chip.
5 Fluffy rough around green: Use your most-lofted wedge (58–60°). Open the face slightly, make a fuller swing, and let the bounce do the work — it won't dig.
Power & Accuracy

Driving the Ball

Distance is exciting, but the fairway is where scoring begins. Learn to hit it long and straight.

💨 Off the Tee

Longer, Straighter Drives

The driver is the only club where you intentionally hit up on the ball. This upward angle of attack — combined with a high tee, slight ball-forward position, and a tilted spine — produces the launch angle and low spin that maximise distance.

Most amateur golfers lose distance not from lack of swing speed, but from poor launch conditions: too much spin, too low a launch, or both. Fix the setup and the distance follows naturally.

1 Tee height: Half the ball should sit above the top edge of the driver face. Tee too low is the single most common cause of pop-up drives.
2 Ball position: Inside the left heel (for right-handers). No further back — this destroys the upward strike you need with the driver.
3 Spine tilt: At address, your right shoulder sits 2–3 inches lower than your left. This naturally promotes an upward attack angle through the hitting zone.
4 Wide arc: Keep the club wide and low during the takeaway. A wide backswing creates a powerful, shallow angle of attack — the opposite of the "over the top" move.
5 Target accuracy: Pick a specific target — not "the fairway." A tree, a bunker, a distant patch of rough. Specificity produces alignment. Generality produces misses.
6 Tempo over tension: Grip pressure stays constant throughout the swing. Tension in the forearms kills clubhead speed. Loose wrists at the top = faster release at impact.
Instant Improvement

5 Tips You Can Use Today

Small adjustments that produce immediate results on the course.

👁️

Look at the Back of the Ball

Focus your gaze on the back of the ball — specifically the spot you intend to hit. This single cue improves centre contact more than almost any other fix.

🫁

Breathe Before You Swing

Take one slow exhale before starting your swing. It lowers heart rate, releases tension in the grip, and activates your parasympathetic system for calmer, more coordinated movement.

🦶

Check Your Foot Alignment

Lay a club along your toe line before every practice session. Most golfers aim well right of their target without knowing it. Your feet should be parallel to the target line, not pointed at the target.

⏸️

Pause at the Top

On the driving range, try pausing for one full second at the top of your backswing before starting down. It eliminates rushing, kills the "over the top" move, and builds a feel for the correct transition.

📍

Pick an Intermediate Target

Find a spot 2–3 feet in front of your ball on the intended line — a discoloured patch of grass, a leaf. Align the clubface to that spot. Short-range alignment is far more accurate than trying to aim at a flag 150 yards away.

🔄

Practice with Purpose

Hitting 100 balls mindlessly does almost nothing. Instead, practise a specific drill for 20–30 balls, then play 10 "course" shots with full pre-shot routines. Quality repetition beats mindless volume every time.
