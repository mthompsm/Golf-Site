A Coach Who Has Been Where You Are

Mike Thompson grew up in Portland, Oregon, where his father introduced him to golf at the age of nine on a battered municipal course. He fell in love instantly — not with the score, but with the puzzle. Why did that shot go left? What made the ball check up on the green? He spent his teenage years seeking answers, spending every weekend on the practice range and reading every instruction book he could find.

After earning a sports science degree from the University of Oregon, Mike pursued his PGA certification and began his teaching career at Pinehurst Ridge Golf Club, where he quickly developed a reputation for clear explanations, endless patience, and a gift for identifying the root cause of a fault — not just the symptom. Within three years he was head of instruction.

In 2012, Mike founded Par Excellence Golf Academy with a single driving bay and a small outdoor putting green. Today, the academy operates out of a state-of-the-art facility featuring four covered teaching bays with high-speed video, a full indoor putting studio, a short-game practice area, and TrackMan launch monitor technology in every bay.

Over his eighteen-year career Mike has taught more than 500 students — from complete beginners who had never held a club, to competitive amateurs, club champions, and several aspiring professionals. His most satisfying lessons are still with the beginners: "Watching someone hit their first pure iron shot — and see their face — never gets old."

Mike competes regularly in regional amateur events and maintains a +3 handicap, which he considers essential to staying current with the game. "I want to face the same challenges my students face," he says. "That keeps my instruction honest."

My Teaching Philosophy

Every golfer is different — different body types, different athletic backgrounds, different goals. Great instruction recognises that and refuses to apply a single template to every student. Mike's approach is built on three pillars:

Understand First

Explain the "why" behind every change. Students who understand why they're doing something retain it. Those who just copy a position forget it by the next round.

Feel Over Position

Golf is played on a course, not in a mirror. Mike prioritises drills that build feel and awareness, so improvements transfer from the range to the course.

One Thing at a Time

Information overload is the enemy of progress. Every lesson focuses on a single key move or concept. Mastery of one thing beats awareness of ten.
