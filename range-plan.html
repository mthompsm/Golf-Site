Structured Practice

The 40-Minute Range Plan

Stop hitting bucket after bucket with no purpose. This session is built around the 7 lessons — designed to build muscle memory, not just burn balls.

Why Most Range Sessions Don't Work

Most golfers go to the range, pull out their driver, and hit balls until their arm hurts. They leave feeling like they "practiced" but nothing changes on the course. That's because hitting balls isn't practice — deliberate repetition of correct fundamentals is practice.

This 40-minute plan structures your session around the fundamentals that matter. You'll spend more time on the things that actually build consistency and less time grooving bad habits through repetition.

The 4 Phases

40 minutes total. Every minute counts.

Phase 1

Grip & Setup

10 minutes · 15–20 balls · 7-iron or 8-iron

Start every single session here. No exceptions. The grip and setup need to become so automatic that you stop thinking about them — and the only way to get there is to deliberately check them every time you practice.

Grip Check (2 min, no hitting)

Before you hit a single ball, grip the club and check: lead hand V toward left shoulder, trail hand V toward right shoulder. Thumbs aligned down the shaft. This is the non-negotiable starting point.

Setup Rehearsal (3 min, slow motion)

Take your address position slowly. Check: bend from the hips, ball close to the body (not reaching), right shoulder slightly back, right foot slightly back, club pointed toward the left hip. Take slow half-swings just to feel the position.

First Balls (5 min)

Hit 15–20 balls with a mid-iron at 60% effort. Focus only on grip and setup — nothing else. Before each shot, do your 4-point reset: grip, distance, shoulder back, turn commitment.

Phase 2

Turn Drills

10 minutes · 15–20 balls · 7-iron or 8-iron

This is the most important phase. The turn is the most important concept in the guide — and the most common place things break down. These drills specifically train the body-turn feeling.

Eyes Closed Swings (3 min)

Hit 8–10 balls with your eyes closed. This sounds strange but it works. Without visual input, your body defaults to feel — and you quickly sense whether your body is turning or whether your arms are doing all the work. Most golfers are shocked by what they feel.

Slow Motion Turn Drill (3 min)

Take the club back in slow motion — 5 seconds for the full backswing — focusing only on the body turn. Feel the left shoulder go back, the right hip staying in place, the weight loading into the right side. Pause at the top. Then swing through normally.

Normal Swings with Turn Focus (4 min)

Hit 10–12 balls at normal tempo with one thought only: "make the turn." Don't think about the arms, the wrist, or the impact. Just commit to a full body rotation and let everything else happen.

Phase 3

Contact Work

10 minutes · 15–20 balls · various irons

You can't fix what you can't see. This phase uses feedback tools to give you instant, objective data on your contact — no guessing, no hoping, just information.

Strike Spray (recommended)

Strike spray (also called foot powder spray) applied to the clubface shows exactly where the ball contacts the face after each shot. Center hits feel and sound different — once you've seen 10 shots, the pattern tells you exactly what to adjust.

Face Tape (alternative)

Impact tape on the face works the same way — stick it on, hit 5 shots, read the marks. Both tools eliminate the guesswork. You're not trying to feel your way to center contact — you're seeing it objectively and adjusting accordingly.

Feedback Session

Hit 15–20 balls across a range of irons (4-iron through 9-iron) using one of the feedback tools. After each set of 5, check the pattern. Are you catching it off the toe? Adjust your distance to the ball. Off the heel? Move slightly further away. Center is the goal.

Phase 4

Full Swings — One Thought Only

10 minutes · 15–20 balls · mixed clubs

The final phase is about trusting the work you just did. No more mechanics. No checklists. Pick one simple swing thought and commit to it for every shot. This trains the transition from practice mindset to on-course mindset.

One Thought

Choose one of these and stick with it for the entire phase — don't switch mid-session:

  • "Make the turn"
  • "Turn, then set"
  • "Smooth tempo"
  • "Stay close"

Simulate the Course

Pick targets on the range. Treat each shot like a real shot on the course — go through your pre-shot routine, pick your target, one swing thought, commit, and swing. Don't rake the next ball immediately. Reset between each shot the way you would on the course.

Finish the Session Strong

End on 5 good swings — doesn't mean perfect results, means you committed fully to the process each time. That's what you're building: commitment to a simple process, not perfection.

Pre-Shot Checklist

Run this before every shot on the range and on the course.

1
Grip correct?

Lead hand V left shoulder. Trail hand V right shoulder. Thumbs together.

2
Ball close — not reaching?

Bend from the hips. Ball inside your arms, not stretched out in front.

3
Shoulder slightly back?

Right shoulder and right foot just slightly back from neutral. Sets up the turn.

4
Committed to a full turn?

One swing thought. Full body rotation. Arms follow, body leads.
