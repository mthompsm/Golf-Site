The 5-Session Range Plan

Five focused range sessions. One concept per visit. Start with the easiest club and build up. Two sessions a week plus actual golf — that's the whole program.

Why Most Range Sessions Don't Work

Most golfers go to the range, grab their driver, and hit balls until their arm hurts. They leave feeling like they practiced — but nothing changes on the course. That's because hitting balls isn't practice. Deliberate repetition of one thing at a time is practice.

This plan gives you five sessions, each with one clear focus. You won't work on your backswing and your release in the same visit. You won't jump between clubs. You'll do one thing, do it well, and leave knowing exactly what you worked on.

The other thing: golf should be fun. Don't turn the range into a second job. Two focused sessions a week plus a round of actual golf is the sweet spot — enough repetition to improve, enough playing to remember why you love the game.

The Golden Rule

If a longer club isn't working, go back to an easier one. Always. There's no shame in spending two sessions on grip and setup if that's what needs work. The progression only moves forward when the current club feels consistent — not before.

Session Detail

Each session is 30–40 minutes. One focus. Don't add more.

Session 1

Grip & Setup

30–40 min · Pitching Wedge & 9-iron only

Everything starts here. Use the most forgiving clubs in your bag so you can focus entirely on how you're holding the club and standing to the ball — not on the result of the shot. Don't move to a longer club today. That's not the point of this session.

Grip Check Before Every Single Shot

Lead hand V toward left shoulder. Trail hand V toward right shoulder. Thumbs aligned down the shaft. Do this standing still, without rushing, before you address the ball. It feels slow. Do it anyway — this is how habits form.

Setup Rehearsal — No Ball

Take your address position 5–10 times without hitting anything. Feel: bend from the hips (not the waist), ball close to your body, right shoulder slightly back, right foot slightly back. You're building a physical memory, not testing your swing.

Hit Balls — Focus Only on Grip and Setup

Hit 20–25 balls with your PW and 9-iron at about 60% effort. Before each shot — stop, check the grip, check the setup, then swing. Ignore the ball flight completely. Where the ball goes is not the point today. The process is the point.

Repeat this session if the grip still doesn't feel automatic by the end. There's no rush to Session 2. A solid grip and setup is worth two extra range visits.
Session 2

The Turn

30–40 min · 9-iron & 8-iron

This is the most important session in the whole plan. The turn is the engine of the swing. Almost every miss — the slice, the push, the fat shot — traces back to not making a full body turn. Give this session your full attention.

Eyes Closed Swings — 10 Balls

Hit 10 balls with your eyes closed. Sounds odd — works every time. Without visual distraction your body defaults to feel, and you'll immediately sense whether your body is turning or your arms are taking over. Most golfers are genuinely surprised by what they feel. Start with your 9-iron.

Slow Motion Turn — 10 Reps

Take the club back in slow motion — 5 full seconds for the backswing. Feel the left shoulder going back, the right hip staying in place, the weight loading into the right side. Pause at the top. Then swing through at normal speed. This is not about hitting a good shot. It's about feeling the turn.

Normal Swings — One Thought Only

Hit 20 balls at normal tempo. One swing thought: "make the turn." Nothing else. Not the wrist, not the arms, not impact. Just commit to rotating your body fully on every swing. Move to your 8-iron for the last 10 balls.

If the 8-iron feels off, go back to the 9-iron. The club progression only matters if the swing feels right. Consistency with a shorter club beats struggling with a longer one every time.
Session 3

Backswing & Tempo

30–40 min · 8-iron & 7-iron

Backswing and tempo belong together because they fix the same problem — rushing. A slow, sequenced backswing and a smooth tempo are two sides of the same coin. Work on them together and they reinforce each other.

The Sequence Drill — Turn Then Set

Take 10 slow practice swings focusing only on the sequence: body turns first, wrists set second. Not at the same time — in order. At the top, check that your lead wrist feels flat, like you're carrying a tray. This is the "tray position." If your wrist is bent back, the face is open.

The 70% Effort Drill

Hit 15 balls swinging at what feels like 70% effort. Not slow — just not max. Notice the contact. Notice the ball flight. Most golfers are surprised how much better the results are at reduced effort. This is your baseline swing. Build from here, not from 100%.

Normal Swings with Tempo Focus

Hit 15–20 balls with your 7-iron. One thought: "slow back, smooth through." Don't try to kill it. Trust the club. A 7-iron hit solidly goes further than a 7-iron hit hard and off-center — every time.

Swinging too hard is almost always caused by not trusting the fundamentals. If you catch yourself over-swinging, slow the backswing down and the tempo usually fixes itself.
Session 4

Release & Contact

30–40 min · 7-iron & 6-iron

This session introduces feedback tools. You've been building the swing from the ground up — now it's time to see what your contact actually looks like. Objective feedback beats guessing every time.

Strike Spray or Face Tape

Apply strike spray (foot powder spray works perfectly) or impact tape to the clubface before hitting. After each shot, look at the mark. Center face is the goal. Off the toe? You're reaching — move slightly closer. Off the heel? Move slightly further away. The feedback is instant and honest.

The Underhand Throw Feel

Before hitting, practice the underhand toss motion — the way you'd pitch something underhanded to someone nearby. That rotating, sweeping motion is exactly what a correct release feels like through the hitting zone. Then hit 10 balls trying to recreate that feeling.

Release Swings — Body Leads

Hit 15–20 balls focused on one thing: body turns through first, hands follow. If your hands feel like they're flipping or over-rotating, your body stopped turning. The fix is always the same — more turn, not different hands. Move to the 6-iron for the last 10 balls.

If the 6-iron isn't working, stay with the 7-iron. No rush. The longer clubs will come — but only after the shorter ones are consistent.
Session 5

Putting It Together

30–40 min · 6-iron down to 5-iron

This is the session where you stop thinking about mechanics and start playing golf. One swing thought. Real targets. Pre-shot routine on every ball. You're training the transition from practice mode to course mode — which is a skill in itself.

Pick One Swing Thought for the Whole Session

Before you hit the first ball, choose one cue and commit to it for the entire session. Don't switch:

  • "Make the turn"
  • "Turn, then set"
  • "Smooth tempo"
  • "Stay close"

Pre-Shot Routine on Every Ball

Pick a target. Run your 4-point checklist: grip, ball close, shoulder back, full turn commitment. One swing thought. Commit. Swing. Then step back before the next shot — don't just rake and hit. The routine is what transfers to the course.

Work Through the Bag

Hit 5 balls with each club: 6-iron, then 5-iron. Same swing, same routine, same thought. If the 5-iron feels off, go back to the 6-iron. Finish on 5 committed swings — doesn't mean perfect, means you committed fully to the process each time.

After Session 5: Go back to whichever session needs the most work and repeat it. The program isn't meant to be done once — it's a framework you return to whenever something breaks down.

Pre-Shot Checklist

Run this before every shot on the range and on the course. It takes 5 seconds and changes everything.

1
Grip correct?

Lead hand V left shoulder. Trail hand V right shoulder. Thumbs together.

2
Ball close — not reaching?

Bend from the hips. Ball inside your arms, not stretched out in front.

3
Shoulder slightly back?

Right shoulder and right foot just slightly back. Sets up the turn.

4
Committed to a full turn?

One swing thought. Full body rotation. Arms follow, body leads.
